Start with the situation the reader recognizes, who acts, and what changes for them. Translate an unfamiliar mechanism into its observable effect. For example, explain what the reader sees when a run pauses, who can release it, and what work waits. Use the product’s actual label when the reader will encounter it, then explain it in ordinary words.

Retain conditions that affect cost, timing, availability, or responsibility. On an audience-changing derivation, omit wiring details that do not affect those expectations. Preserve uncertainty and approvals even when a simpler story would be easier to tell.
